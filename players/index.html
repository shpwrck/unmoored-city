The Unmoored City: Player Guide

Everything on this page is public. Your character's private motive card is handed to you at the table and is yours alone.

The premise the players know

Beyond a broken ravine lies an ancient ruined city that sometimes appears intact through silver Mist. The Mist can pull a plausible finished future into the present: a repaired bridge, a forged tool, a working cistern. Stories disagree about what the city loses when it skips the work between intention and result. Each character has a public reason to investigate and one private opportunity. The road home becomes real after the opening experiment, and it reappears at every return to the central square.

The repeated expedition loop

This test contains five complete expedition cycles:

  1. Choose one unfinished project and learn its known reward, principle, and hazard.
  2. Explore that project's site.
  3. Observe, test, negotiate, bypass, fight, use Mist, or propose another plausible route.
  4. Return with a tangible resource, operating knowledge, and any evidence recovered.
  5. Take an optional Short Rest and settle public custody.
  6. Build the project by spending one visible Foreclock interval, or leave while the road remains open.

The two anchor characters retain a complete route through every puzzle and finale task. A character sheet can make a route faster, safer, or more expressive, but no class, spell, tool, species, motive, or optional player is required.

The Foreclock

The Foreclock has six visible intervals. The opening experiment spends the first. Each completed project spends one more. The Steward and overlapping city become more coherent as the intervals darken. The fifth project spends the sixth interval and begins Reclamation.

At each of the first four project builds, a Three-Hand Commissioning Dial makes that accounting a small public puzzle instead of hidden bookkeeping. Its three hands show completed returns, built projects, and spent Foreclock intervals. The build lock opens only at returns=N; built=N; Foreclock spent=N+1. A wrong alignment gives visible mismatch feedback and may be corrected freely; it spends no action, roll, resource, interval, or consequence. The fifth build uses the separate Forked-Key Commissioning Gate after the outcome audit.

The DM states the known consequence before every project pull. Before the final pull, the DM discloses every supported ending and asks the active players to restate the stakes. No opening project preference commits the party to a later pull.

Before that final choice, the table completes the Six-Seal Outcome Audit. Six public seals—Living Anchor, Full Transfer, Partial Transfer, Erasure, Voluntary Anchor, and Capacity—are matched against the visible Leave, Full Transfer, Partial Transfer, and Voluntary Anchor columns. The outcome matrix stays in view; this verifies informed consent rather than testing memory. Correct placement needs no roll. The follow-up False-Seal Ledger puzzle adds one counterfeit claim—“Consent can replace missing task capacity.” Each anchor separately says counterfeit rejected, restates the six real boundaries, and places a distinct signature key in that anchor's rail. A mistake sounds two notes and the DM restates the missing row; both independent rails must latch before a complete audit sounds one clear note and records public-outcome-proof.

The proof lets either player request one free delta-only reminder of the currently applicable outcome row during the finale. It never supplies an action, task, progress, Doubt, capacity, or reroll.

The final BUILD/LEAVE decision uses a Forked-Key Commissioning Gate. Each anchor has one independent key with BUILD and LEAVE faces; one key cannot operate the other rail. After the DM opens the gate, each player names one specific stake in the form personal future stake: what BUILD tests, preserves, costs, or tempts and turns only that character's key. Both BUILD faces are required for the final pull. If either player chooses LEAVE, the road remains open and Reclamation does not begin.

Before turning a key, a character may optionally test or personalize that rail with a relevant class feature, skill, tool, or spell. A sound inspection needs no roll; genuinely uncertain execution uses one DC 10 check. Success earns one commissioning edge for Advantage on a later related Reclamation Influence, Study, or Utilize check. Failure still proves the rails independent and leaves the choice open, though any declared action or resource remains spent. The edge cannot create task progress, Doubt, or capacity or bypass a later-round gate.

The project board

At every return, choose any unfinished row. The order is strategic and changes what tools are available during later expeditions. Your table may offer only the core five, or the wider board.

The core five

Project and arena Public capability Recoverable principle Known hazard
Surveyor's Lens: Counterweight Foundry Separates ruined, restored, and parallel structures; exposes false routes. The lift must satisfy both total load and leverage while reserving enough mass for an inspection sling. A living operator stays outside the cage. Dormant bronze sentinels defend forced or exposed operation.
Refuge Gate: Floodglass Cistern Holds a safe boundary and gives endangered people a stable way out. Ten water-marks must be divided exactly while separated operators translate an old pressure-sign system and brace the correct grate before each pour. Corrosive gray oozes wake when overflow reaches an unbraced grate.
Handwork Forge: Emberglass Kiln Lets living craftspeople inspect, repair, and correct Mist-assisted work. Six single-use controls must produce the shape, anneal, and release states in order; some are latched, and one changes heat proportionally instead of by a fixed amount. Bound fire and steam elementals escape when a latch, vent, or reset is forced.
Witness Bell: Echo Archive Authenticates records and carries one documented truth through temporal overlap. A valid record chain must agree in seals, custody, dates, and living witnesses; a plausible office copy is not enough. Rival salvagers and disturbed bat swarms contest custody.
Meridian Spire: Meridian Orrery Distinguishes the current, remembered, and freely possible skies and marks stable passage. The correct three-sky geometry must be reached through coupled wheels within the web-tension limit, without copying the remembered sky. Web predators occupy the moving rings and wake when the tension gauge reaches red.

Each beast has a want and a fear on its old pen plaque; a beast led to its want and away from its fear follows the ring-bearer. Pens seal only around a pair. Three pairs of unmoored beasts, fractious and half-present, in three warrens.

Every project remains useful if the party stops early. Completing a project expands what the base can do; it does not silently complete a later finale action.

Return boundaries and table rotation

The central square is the only join, leave, and custody-transfer boundary. At a return:

  • an arriving player receives the public recap, one unclaimed character sheet, and that character's private motive choice;
  • a departing player returns the character sheet and private dossier to the DM;
  • cargo and public evidence go to the visible base ledger unless an active character explicitly keeps custody; and
  • the two anchors keep every critical route.

Finale expectations

The finale uses initiative, enemies, terrain, four public anchor tasks, persuasion, attacks, rescue, movement, tools, spells, and other ordinary D&D actions. Projects make specific procedures reliable, but the players still spend actions to perform them.

The four tasks are Map, Fabricate, Brace, and Synchronize. A full sound route uses five progress actions because Synchronize requires Calibrate and then Lock in later rounds. The Witness Bell supports Influence against the Steward; it is not a fifth task and does not add task progress.

The public contract distinguishes these stakes: living people can be fixed in stasis and lose departure and future change; four tasks and five progress actions support full transfer of the five-project boundary; exactly three tasks support only one player-selected district or named group; fewer than three tasks cannot support transfer and can erase supported Unmoored when the Steward is dispersed; partial transfer erases unsupported Unmoored; a character can voluntarily anchor at the same permanent personal cost; and Doubt or persuasion changes the Steward's conduct but cannot replace task capacity. A full transfer also leaves all five project instruments behind as permanent civic boundary supports.

After Initiative, the DM stops after every round, publishes one authoritative ROUND CARD, and asks each player separately what that character does next. The card shows current TS, every remaining limited resource, current healing formulas, all station aliases, exact character positions, active opponents with Cohesion and positions, all task and later-round gate states, exact Doubt, Movement exposure: for adjacent Opportunity Attack and cover consequences, Next Progress Gate: for what can advance now and what opens next, and Legal next actions:. Round numbers begin at 1 and advance by one. An opening plan cannot authorize several future rounds. This gives the table room to react to damage, the Steward's conduct, changing capacity, and one another.

The exact station aliases are CENTER = Meridian Spire, NORTH = Surveyor's Lens, EAST = Handwork Forge, SOUTH = Refuge Gate, and WEST = Witness Bell. Every outer project housing is 20 feet from CENTER. The neighboring outer edges NORTH-EAST, EAST-SOUTH, SOUTH-WEST, and WEST-NORTH are 20 feet. The non-neighboring outer pairs NORTH-SOUTH and EAST-WEST are 30 feet. A housing grants Half Cover only when it physically stands between attacker and target, never against an adjacent attacker sharing that housing.

Before Initiative, the new Five-Lane Route Card puzzle verifies that geometry. Jesse independently confirms WEST-NORTH = neighboring = 20 feet and EAST-WEST = non-neighboring = 30 feet. Frank independently confirms NORTH-EAST = neighboring = 20 feet and NORTH-SOUTH = non-neighboring = 30 feet. A wrong key sounds two notes and is freely retryable; it costs no action, roll, resource, Doubt, progress, capacity, or time, and one player cannot place the other player's key.

Table expectations

  • Break character naturally. Jokes, rules questions, theories, and reactions are welcome.
  • If two of you want the same exclusive job, each give a first and second choice once. If the first choices still collide, agree on a tie-break, and whoever wins it keeps priority for that arena. Nobody controls anyone else's character.
  • Test the premise. Try the apparently wrong way. Circumvent the expected route if it is credible. I will adjudicate from the stated physical and magical principles.
  • A correct feasible procedure succeeds without a gratuitous check.
  • Once everyone has authorized a complete safe procedure and their own role, I will resolve the deterministic steps together. Play pauses again for any new clue, NPC, hazard, resource choice, changed mechanism, or decision.
  • Failed checks and wrong models change cost, position, pressure, or information. They do not erase the expedition.
  • Combat is a legitimate route, never the only one.
  • Mist is a tempting shortcut with a visible anomaly and a repair path.
  • You may disagree, reveal motives, or pursue self-interest, but nobody controls another player's character or hides a critical public clue.

The twelve characters

All twelve are level 3, built on the standard array with SRD backgrounds and species. Every critical puzzle and finale task has a route open to any two of them; no class, spell, or tool is required.

Choosing a character

  1. Read the public pitches below. Each character has a stable public disposition and a public reason for coming to the ravine.
  2. The two anchor players choose first, and must choose different characters. A player joining later takes any unclaimed sheet.
  3. After you choose, the DM privately offers you three motive cards for that character. You keep one and tell only the DM. Your public disposition does not change, so nobody learns your choice unless you reveal it.
  4. Your motive may create negotiation at the table. It never requires betrayal, theft from another player, or hiding a clue the party needs.
  5. There is no Long Rest during the one-shot. Anything that returns on a Long Rest is once for the day. A Short Rest is offered every time you return to the square.
