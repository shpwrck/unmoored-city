The Unmoored City: Player Guide

A guide to choosing your character and playing at the table. Your character's private motive card comes from the DM and is yours to keep secret or share.

Your adventure begins

Beyond a broken ravine lies an ancient ruined city that sometimes appears intact through silver Mist. The Mist can pull a plausible finished future into the present: a repaired bridge, a forged tool, a working cistern. Stories disagree about what the city loses when it skips the work between intention and result. Your character has a reason to investigate and an opportunity they hope to find there.

Exploring and returning home

The central square is your home between expeditions. Choose a project together, explore the place that holds what it needs, and bring your discoveries back to help restore the city.

  1. Ask about the unfinished projects the DM offers. Decide which benefit the group wants next.
  2. Explore, ask questions, inspect things, and describe what your character tries. You can negotiate, use your skills and equipment, look for another way through, or fight.
  3. Bring back useful materials, knowledge, and clues. Agree who is carrying important items.
  4. Back at the square, share what you learned, take an optional Short Rest, and decide together what to build next.

The adventure supports two continuing players, with room for others to join. Choose a character you want to play; the group does not need a particular class or spell to proceed.

The Mist and the Foreclock

The Foreclock shows six visible intervals. The opening use of the Mist spends the first, and building a project spends another. Watch it as the city changes around you.

Mist can offer a tempting shortcut. Ask what your character knows about its cost and what signs you can inspect afterward. You can investigate, suggest another approach, or decide against using it.

The DM explains known risks before you commit to a project and explains lasting consequences before a major choice. Ask for a recap whenever you need one. Choosing a project now does not decide your later choices for you.

Joining, leaving, and sharing equipment

New characters join the group when it returns to the central square. If you are arriving or leaving, let the DM know so there is time for a recap and a handoff.

  • When you join, choose an unclaimed character and receive your private motive from the DM. Ask the other players what has happened so far.
  • When you leave, return your sheet and motive card to the DM. Make sure the remaining group has the clues and shared items it needs.
  • At each return, agree which supplies belong at the base and which character is carrying anything the group needs on its next trip.

Playing together

  • Jokes, rules questions, theories, and reactions are welcome. You do not need to stay in character all the time.
  • Describe what you want to do and how you try it. Ask the DM when a rule or ability is unfamiliar; you do not need to know every rule before playing.
  • Try ideas that make sense in the scene, even if they seem different from the obvious approach. Combat is an option, and so are negotiation, exploration, and practical problem-solving.
  • Give everyone room to speak. If two people want the same job, talk it through and agree who tries it first.
  • Make choices for your own character. You can disagree or pursue a private ambition without controlling another character or hiding a clue the group needs.
  • When it is your turn, say what you want to do now. You can change your plans as the situation changes.
  • Keep track of damage, ammunition, spell slots, and limited-use abilities on your sheet. Ask for help with the bookkeeping whenever you need it.

The twelve characters

All twelve characters are level 3. Read their pitches and choose someone whose abilities and reason for visiting the city appeal to you.

Choosing a character

  1. The two players staying for the whole adventure choose first and choose different characters. Anyone joining later takes an unclaimed sheet.
  2. The DM privately offers you three motive cards for your character. Choose one and tell the DM. The public description stays the same; you decide whether to reveal your private motive.
  3. A motive can give you something to negotiate for. It does not require betraying another player, stealing from them, or hiding a clue the group needs.
  4. There is no Long Rest during this one-shot. Anything that returns on a Long Rest is available only once for the day. You can take a Short Rest each time the group returns to the square.
